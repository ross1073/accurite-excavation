## Facts the writer may use

- NOAA says a **strong El Niño is underway** and is forecast to persist through
  **January–March 2027** (source 1). The Nov 12 ENSO discussion puts the chance of it
  staying **strong to very strong through Jan–Mar 2027 at greater than 79%** (source 4).
- NOAA expects El Niño to be the major influence on U.S. winter weather this year
  (source 1).
- Nationally, El Niño tilts the odds toward a **wetter southern U.S.** (California, the
  Southwest, the Gulf Coast, the Southeast and East Coast) and a **warmer northern U.S.**
  (source 1, source 3).
- **Northern Utah — Dec–Feb precipitation: equal chances** of a wetter, normal or drier
  winter. Not forecast wetter. Not forecast drier. (source 2, Dec–Feb precip map; source 3
  "Intermountain West … equal chances").
- **Northern Utah — Dec–Feb temperature: equal chances** (source 2, Dec–Feb temp map).
- **Northern Utah — Jan–Mar 2027 temperature: leaning slightly warmer** (33–40% chance of
  above normal; source 2, Jan–Mar temp map). Jan–Mar precipitation: northern Utah is
  equal chances, with the "leaning wetter" band starting just to the south (source 2).
- To the north, NOAA favors a **drier-than-normal winter for the northern Rockies**
  (source 1), and drought is favored to develop or worsen in parts of the northern
  Rockies (source 3).
- NOAA does not forecast seasonal snowfall amounts; it says snow is "generally not
  predictable more than a week in advance" (source 3).

## Inferences — must be phrased with "can" / "could" / "if", never "will"

These are general physical relationships, **not NOAA forecasts for Utah**:

- *If* the winter runs warm, precipitation that would have been snow **can** fall as rain
  instead, and that water hits the ground right away instead of waiting for spring.
- Warm spells in winter **can** mean more freeze-thaw cycles, which work on soil,
  gravel roads, culvert ends and retaining-wall backfill.
- A warm late winter **can** bring snowmelt and runoff earlier than usual.
- Whatever the season does, Northern Utah clay soils drain slowly and water that has
  nowhere to go collects against foundations and walls (the site's existing
  avoid-flooding post makes the same point).

## Hard "do not say" list

- Do NOT say El Niño storms, a wet winter, flooding, or heavy snow **will** come (or are
  "expected" / "forecast" / "likely") in Utah. The Utah forecast is a coin flip.
- Do NOT say NOAA forecasts a warmer Dec–Feb for Northern Utah (it is equal chances).
- Do NOT mention Salt Lake City or Salt Lake County.
- Do NOT name, quote or paraphrase any other contractor's or consultant's newsletter.
